answer: Marketing attribution is the process of assigning credit for conversions to the channels and campaigns that influenced them. It combines consistent tracking and UTM tagging, attribution models in GA4 and ad platforms, CRM data on full customer journeys and, where possible, incrementality tests that show what would have happened without a channel.

## What Marketing Attribution covers

> Last-click reporting tends to overvalue brand search and undervalue the channels that created demand.

We tidy UTM tagging, compare attribution views across GA4, ad platforms and your CRM, and run simple holdout or geo tests where budgets allow. The output is a clear view of which spend drives growth.

Baymard Institute's running average across 50 studies puts online cart abandonment at about 70 percent. Not all of it is fixable, but slow pages, surprise costs, long forms and missing proof are. Every improvement in conversion makes every other marketing channel cheaper per lead.

## How we deliver it {wide}

^ The process

::: steps
- **Measure.** Fix tracking and set a reliable baseline.
- **Research.** Analytics, recordings, surveys and expert review.
- **Hypothesise.** What to change, why, and the result we expect.
- **Test.** A/B tests where traffic allows, careful comparison where it does not.
- **Roll out.** Ship winners, record learning, move to the next idea.
:::

## Questions about Marketing Attribution {wide alt}

^ FAQ

::: faq
? Which attribution model is right?
No model is perfect. Comparing several and testing incrementality gives a more honest picture.

? What is data-driven attribution?
GA4 and Google Ads models that assign credit based on observed conversion paths in your data.

? Do we need a CRM for attribution?
It helps a lot for lead-based businesses, because sales happen after the website visit.
:::
