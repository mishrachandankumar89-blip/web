## Why most content does nothing

> The internet does not need another article that summarises the first page of Google.

Google's guidance on helpful content asks three questions of every page: who created it, how it was created, and why. Pages that exist mainly to attract search traffic, summarise others without adding anything, or are produced at scale with little oversight are exactly what its systems now push down. Since the March 2024 core update, Google has also had an explicit spam policy against scaled content abuse.

AI assistants have raised the bar further. The GEO research presented at KDD 2024 found that adding statistics, quotations and citations to credible sources increased how often generative engines used a page. In other words, the content that gets cited is content with evidence in it.

So we write less, and we write with more substance. Every piece starts with a real question and ends with something a reader can act on.

## How we build content that earns its place

### Start from questions, not keywords

We collect the questions your buyers ask from Search Console, sales calls, support tickets, forums and AI tools, then group them by buying stage. Keywords inform the language. Questions decide what we write.

### Put real experience on the page

Google's quality rater guidelines added Experience to E-A-T in December 2022, making E-E-A-T. In practice that means first-hand detail: what your team has seen on real projects, the mistakes clients make, the numbers from your own work. We interview your specialists for 20 to 30 minutes per piece and turn that into writing that sounds like them.

### Bring evidence

Statistics with sources, quotes from named people, tables that compare options, screenshots and original data where possible. Every factual claim links to the primary source, so readers and AI systems can check it.

### Structure for scanning and quoting

A direct answer at the top, clear headings that match questions, short paragraphs, lists for steps and tables for comparisons. Long articles get a table of contents. Each section should still make sense if an assistant quotes it alone.

## Our editorial process {wide}

^ The process

::: steps
- **Brief.** Search intent, questions to answer, competing pages, sources to check and the expert to interview.
- **Interview.** A short call with your specialist to capture experience, examples and opinions.
- **Draft.** Written by a subject-aware writer, answer first, with sources linked.
- **Edit and fact-check.** Every claim checked against its source, every number dated.
- **Optimise.** Titles, headings, internal links, schema and images with descriptive alt text.
- **Publish and update.** Performance reviewed after 90 days, then refreshed as facts change.
:::

## How we use AI, and how we do not

We use AI tools for research support, outlines, summarising long sources and checking coverage against competing pages. We do not publish AI drafts as finished content, and we do not use AI to produce pages at volume. Every piece is written or rewritten by a person, checked against primary sources and reviewed before publication. That is better for readers, safer under Google's spam policies and more likely to be cited.

| Approach | Typical result |
|---|---|
| Mass-produced articles on every keyword | Thin pages, index bloat, risk under scaled content abuse policy |
| Fewer, deeper pieces with expert input | Stronger rankings, links earned naturally, AI citations |
| Publishing and forgetting | Slow decay as facts and competitors change |
| Regular refresh of top pages | Rankings held, answers kept current for AI tools |

## Questions about content marketing {wide alt}

^ FAQ

::: faq
? How many articles should we publish each month?
Fewer than most agencies suggest. Two to four well-researched pieces a month, plus updates to older pages, beats twenty thin ones. The right number depends on your market and how much expertise your team can share.

? Will you write in our brand voice?
Yes. We build a short voice guide from your existing material and calls, and every draft is reviewed by you before it goes live.

? Can you write in Hindi or other Indian languages?
Yes, for pages where your buyers search in those languages. We plan language versions with proper hreflang setup so each version reaches the right audience.

? How do you measure content performance?
Rankings and traffic for each piece, enquiries and assisted conversions in GA4, links earned, and whether the page is cited in AI answers for its main question.
:::
