A few years ago, the question every business asked was "how do we rank on page one?" In 2026 there is a second question beside it: "what does the AI say about us?"

The answer matters because people now ask AI assistants for recommendations they used to search for themselves. At Google I/O in May 2026, Google said AI Overviews reach more than 2.5 billion people a month and that AI Mode had passed one billion monthly users. OpenAI said ChatGPT passed one billion weekly users in 2026, and Sam Altman has said India is ChatGPT's second-largest market, with more than 100 million weekly users.

This guide explains what Generative Engine Optimization is, what the evidence says actually works, and how to start without wasting money on tricks.

## Where the term GEO comes from

The phrase was coined in a 2023 research paper, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization", by Pranjal Aggarwal, Vishvak Murahari, Tanmay Rajpurohit, Ashwin Kalyan, Karthik Narasimhan and Ameet Deshpande, from Princeton, Georgia Tech, the Allen Institute for AI and IIT Delhi. It was presented at KDD 2024, one of the leading data science conferences.

The researchers called AI search systems "generative engines": tools that retrieve web pages and then use a language model to write an answer from them. They built a benchmark called GEO-bench, with around 10,000 queries across many subjects, and tested nine ways of editing a web page to see which made the engine use and cite it more.

## What the research found

The headline result was that the right edits could raise a source's visibility in generative answers by up to 40 percent. More useful than the number is which edits worked and which did not.

| Method tested | What it means | Effect in the study |
|---|---|---|
| Statistics addition | Adding relevant numbers and data | Among the strongest gains |
| Quotation addition | Adding quotes from credible people | Among the strongest gains |
| Cite sources | Linking claims to reliable sources | Strong gains |
| Fluency and readability | Clearer, easier writing | Moderate gains |
| Keyword stuffing | Repeating target terms | Little or no benefit |

The lesson is simple and a little old-fashioned. Generative engines favour content that a careful human editor would also trust: specific, sourced, clearly written and useful. The tactic that once fooled early search engines, repeating keywords, did not help.

The paper also found that results varied by subject. A legal question, a health question and a shopping question rewarded slightly different edits. That is why a GEO plan should start from your own buyers' questions, not a generic checklist.

## How AI assistants choose what to cite

Each assistant works differently, and none publishes a full recipe. But the general pattern is consistent enough to plan around:

1. **The question arrives.** Often longer and more specific than a typical search.
2. **The system searches.** Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may use a "query fan-out" technique, running several related searches across subtopics before answering. ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Copilot also retrieve live web results.
3. **It selects passages.** From the results, it keeps the passages that best answer each part of the question.
4. **It writes an answer.** A language model combines those passages into one reply.
5. **It credits sources.** Pages whose information was used are linked or named.

![Diagram of five steps from question to citation](img:diagram-flow "A simplified view of how AI assistants build an answer.")

Two things follow from this. First, **you cannot be cited if you cannot be found**: your pages must be crawlable and indexed. Google says pages need to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet to appear as links in AI Overviews and AI Mode. Second, **the passage that answers the question is what gets used**: a page with a clear, well-supported answer to each question beats a long page that never quite answers anything. You do not need to chop pages into fragments; Google's May 2026 guide says its systems can pick the relevant passage out of a longer page.

## GEO is built on SEO, not instead of it

It is tempting to treat GEO as a new discipline that replaces search optimization. It does not. Google's documentation on AI features says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations for appearing in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and its May 2026 guide to generative AI search says SEO best practices remain relevant because those features are rooted in Google's core ranking and quality systems. Other assistants, such as ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Copilot, run their own indexes, which is why GEO also looks beyond Google.

What GEO adds is a change in emphasis:

- **From rankings to citations.** The goal is to be named in the answer, not just listed below it.
- **From keywords to questions.** Plan content around the real questions buyers ask.
- **From your site to the whole web.** AI tools weigh what other trusted sites say about you.
- **From traffic to presence.** Many AI answers end without a click, so being named has value of its own.

Pew Research Center's 2025 study shows why that last point matters. When Google showed an AI summary, users clicked a traditional search result on 8 percent of visits, compared with 15 percent when there was no summary. They clicked a link inside the summary on only 1 percent of visits.

## A step-by-step GEO plan

### Step 1: Write down the questions that matter

List 50 to 200 questions your buyers ask before they choose a provider. Use Search Console queries, People Also Ask boxes, sales call notes, support emails, reviews and forums. Group them by stage: learning, comparing, deciding.

### Step 2: Measure your starting point

Ask each question in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and Google. Record which brands are named, which pages are cited and whether anything said about you is wrong. Repeat on a schedule, because answers change. Since June 2026, Google Search Console has also offered a generative AI performance report showing how often your pages appear as links in AI Overviews and AI Mode.

### Step 3: Fix access and facts

Make sure search and answer crawlers can reach your pages. Check robots.txt, firewall and CDN rules for user agents such as Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot. Add structured data for your organization and services. Make your company description, services, locations and contact details identical everywhere they appear.

### Step 4: Rewrite key pages answer-first

For each priority question, open the relevant page with a direct 40 to 60 word answer. Follow it with evidence: data, examples, comparisons, quotes from your experts and links to primary sources. Use headings that match the questions and tables for anything that compares options.

### Step 5: Create evidence others want to cite

Original data, clear pricing explainers, benchmarks, calculators and expert guides give AI systems and journalists a reason to reference you. One genuinely new piece of information is worth more than ten summaries of what is already online.

### Step 6: Earn mentions on trusted sites

Digital PR, expert commentary, reviews and listings on respected industry sites build the outside confirmation that AI assistants rely on. Avoid bought links and fake reviews; they break platform rules and add nothing credible.

### Step 7: Track, learn and repeat

Re-run your question set monthly. Watch your share of citations against named competitors, the accuracy of what assistants say about you, and enquiries from AI referrals in Google Analytics 4.

## Common GEO mistakes

- **Chasing hacks.** Hidden prompts, invisible text and keyword-stuffed "AI pages" do not hold up and can damage trust.
- **Blocking the wrong crawlers.** A blanket firewall rule against bots can quietly remove you from AI search results.
- **Ignoring facts about the business.** If your address, services or founding details differ across sites, AI tools may repeat the wrong version.
- **Writing for machines.** Content that reads badly for people also tends to be passed over by AI systems.
- **Expecting overnight results.** Assistants that search live can pick up changes within days; answers based on a model's training data change only when it is retrained.

## How long does GEO take?

For assistants that search the web live, improvements can appear within days or weeks of a page being crawled and indexed. Building the outside authority that makes AI tools trust you takes months, much like SEO. A realistic plan expects a first baseline and fixes in month one, the first citation gains in months two to three, and steadier gains from month four onward.

## Frequently asked questions

::: faq
? Is GEO only about ChatGPT?
No. It covers any system that writes answers from web sources: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot, Claude and others. Each has its own index and quirks, so measure them separately.

? Do I need special schema for AI?
There is no AI-only schema. Standard structured data such as Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, Product, Article and FAQPage helps machines understand your pages, as long as it matches what is visible.

? Can small businesses benefit from GEO?
Yes, often faster than large ones, because many local and niche questions have few well-supported answers. A clear, sourced page can become the go-to reference in a small category quickly.

? Does llms.txt help with GEO?
It is a low-cost addition that some tools may read, but it is a proposed standard, not a ranking factor. Google's May 2026 guide says an llms.txt file gets no special treatment in Search. Accessible, well-structured pages matter far more.
:::
