The short answer

What is the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?

SEO helps your pages rank in search results. AEO shapes content so search engines and voice assistants can lift a direct answer from it, such as a featured snippet. GEO aims to get your brand cited inside AI-written answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews. Each builds on the one before it.

New acronyms appear in marketing every year, and most fade. SEO, AEO and GEO are different. They describe three real ways people now find information: by clicking results, by reading direct answers, and by asking an AI assistant to do the reading for them.

The confusion comes from the overlap. All three depend on good pages. All three reward clarity and trust. But they aim at different places on the screen, and they are measured differently. This guide sets them side by side.

The short version

SEO: earning a place in the results

Search Engine Optimization is the oldest of the three and still the foundation. It covers everything that helps a page rank: matching what people search for, making pages technically easy to crawl and index, writing content that answers the search better than competitors, and earning links and mentions from trusted sites.

Google's own guidance on hiring an SEO says improvements usually take four months to a year to show their full benefit. Since the March 2024 core update, Google has also been firm about quality. It folded its helpful content system into core ranking and added spam policies against scaled content abuse, site reputation abuse and expired domain abuse, and said the changes cut low-quality, unoriginal content in results by about 45 percent.

Measure it with: rankings for commercial searches, organic sessions, and most importantly enquiries and sales from organic traffic in Google Analytics 4.

AEO: becoming the answer on the page

Answer Engine Optimization grew up with featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes and voice assistants. In each case the search engine pulls one passage from a page and shows it as the answer. AEO is the craft of writing and structuring content so your passage is the one chosen.

The tactics are editorial: find the exact question, answer it plainly in the first sentence or two, then support it with detail, lists, tables and examples. Structured data helps machines read the page, although Google limited FAQ rich results to well-known government and health sites in 2023, so markup alone is not a shortcut.

Measure it with: featured snippets and People Also Ask placements won and lost, impressions on question searches in Search Console, and enquiries from the pages that win them.

GEO: being cited by AI

Generative Engine Optimization is the newest. The term comes from a 2023 research paper presented at KDD 2024, which tested how to make web content more visible in AI-generated answers. Adding statistics, quotations and citations to credible sources increased visibility the most, by up to 40 percent in its tests. Keyword stuffing did not help.

GEO goes beyond your own pages. AI assistants form a view of your brand from everything they can find: your site, review platforms, directories, press coverage, social profiles and forums. So GEO includes making your business facts consistent everywhere, earning mentions on sites AI tools trust, and making sure the crawlers behind ChatGPT search, Perplexity and others are allowed to read your pages.

Measure it with: a fixed set of buyer questions run through each assistant monthly, tracking how often you are cited compared with competitors and whether the answers about you are accurate. Google Search Console has offered a generative AI performance report for AI Overviews and AI Mode since June 2026.

How the three fit together

Think of them as floors of one building:

  1. SEO is the ground floor. If search engines cannot find, read and trust your pages, nothing else works. Google says pages need to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode.
  2. AEO is the first floor. Once pages rank, clear answers and structure let engines quote them directly.
  3. GEO is the top floor. With strong pages and clear answers in place, outside mentions and consistent facts make AI assistants confident enough to name you.

Skipping floors rarely works. A business that pours money into "AI optimization" on a site with indexing problems is building a penthouse on sand.

Which one should you invest in first?

Use these questions as a quick guide:

  • Are your important pages indexed and loading fast? If not, start with technical SEO. Everything else depends on it.
  • Do you rank on page one for your main services? If not, SEO comes first: intent research, content and authority.
  • Do you rank, but competitors own the snippets and answer boxes? Focus on AEO: rewrite your top pages answer-first.
  • Do you rank well, but AI assistants recommend competitors? Invest in GEO: evidence, outside mentions and consistent brand facts.
  • Do AI tools describe your business wrongly? GEO again, starting with fixing facts across every profile and directory.

For most established businesses in 2026, the honest answer is a blend: keep SEO strong, apply AEO to the pages that matter most, and add a GEO programme on top.

What does not change

Strip away the acronyms and the advice converges. Google's guidance on helpful content asks who created a page, how and why. Answer engines reward the clearest, best-supported reply. AI assistants cite sources they can find and trust. In every case, the businesses that win are the ones that know their subject, say it clearly and prove it.

Frequently asked questions

Is AEO just another name for GEO?

They overlap, but they target different features. AEO focuses on answer boxes, snippets and voice replies in search engines. GEO focuses on citations in AI-written answers. In practice, good AEO work makes GEO easier.

Will GEO replace SEO?

Not in the foreseeable future. AI assistants and Google's AI features draw on indexed web pages, so SEO remains the entry ticket. GEO changes what you optimise for once you are in.

Can one agency do all three?

Yes, and it usually works better when one team owns the whole stack, because the same pages, facts and authority serve all three goals.

Which is cheapest to start with?

AEO on pages that already rank is often the quickest, lowest-cost win, because it is mostly rewriting and restructuring existing content.
