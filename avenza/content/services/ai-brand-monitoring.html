The short answer

What is AI brand monitoring?

AI brand monitoring is the ongoing tracking of how AI assistants describe and recommend your business. Each month we re-run a fixed set of brand and category questions, record sentiment, accuracy and citations, compare you with competitors, and flag changes that need action, such as a wrong fact or a lost recommendation.

N° 01

What AI Brand Monitoring covers

Reputation used to mean reviews and press. Now it also means what an AI assistant says when someone asks about you by name.

We track brand questions such as 'Is AVENZA reliable?' alongside category questions such as 'best agencies for local SEO in Pune'. When an assistant repeats an error, we trace it to its source and fix it there, because AI tools usually inherit mistakes from the web.

At Google I/O in May 2026, Google said AI Overviews reach more than 2.5 billion people a month and AI Mode has passed one billion monthly users. Pew Research Center found that when Google showed an AI summary, people clicked a traditional result on 8 percent of visits instead of 15 percent. Being named inside the answer now matters as much as ranking below it.

The process

  1. Question map The buyer questions that matter, grouped by stage and value.
  2. Baseline Each question run across the main assistants, with every brand and source cited.
  3. Foundation Crawler access, indexing, structured data and one set of brand facts.

  5. Track The same questions re-run monthly, reported as share of citations.

FAQ

Questions about AI Brand Monitoring

Can you remove wrong information from ChatGPT?

Not directly. We correct the sources the assistant relies on, such as directories, profiles and articles, and track the answer until it changes.

Which assistants do you monitor?

ChatGPT, Google Gemini, AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot as standard, with others on request.

How is this different from social listening?

Social listening tracks what people post. AI monitoring tracks what assistants tell people, which is increasingly where decisions start.
