The short answer

How do you migrate a website without losing SEO?

Migrating without losing SEO depends on mapping every old URL to its closest new URL, testing 301 redirects before launch, keeping important content, metadata and structured data, and verifying that robots rules, canonicals and tracking work on the new site. After launch, indexing, rankings and errors are monitored daily until things settle.

What Website Migration SEO covers

Most migration losses are preventable: missing redirects, deleted content, blocked crawling left over from staging, or broken tracking.

We join the project early, build the URL map from analytics and crawl data, test redirects on staging, compare old and new pages for content parity, and run a launch checklist. Then we monitor closely for several weeks and fix issues as they appear.

Google's own guide to hiring an SEO says improvements usually take four months to a year to show their full benefit. Since its March 2024 core update, Google says it has cut low-quality, unoriginal content in results by about 45 percent, so the pages that win now are specific, useful and technically sound.

FAQ

Questions about Website Migration SEO

When should SEO be involved in a redesign?

At the start. Decisions about URLs, content and templates are cheaper to get right before build.

Will traffic dip after migration?

A short wobble is common while Google reprocesses the site. A well-run migration recovers quickly.

How long should redirects stay in place?

As long as possible, ideally permanently, because links to old URLs keep existing.
