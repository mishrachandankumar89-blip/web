The short answer

What is conversion rate optimization?

Conversion rate optimization (CRO) is the process of increasing the share of website visitors who take a valuable action, such as enquiring, booking or buying. It combines analytics, behaviour recordings, user research and expert review to find friction, then tests clearer copy, layouts, offers, forms and checkout steps to prove what improves results.

N° 01

Why conversion is the cheapest growth lever

Every improvement in conversion makes every other channel more profitable.

If your site turns 2 percent of visitors into enquiries and you raise that to 3 percent, every rupee spent on SEO, ads and social now brings half as many enquiries again. Paid campaigns get cheaper per lead, and organic traffic is worth more.

The friction is usually measurable. Baymard Institute's running average across 50 studies puts online cart abandonment at about 70 percent. On lead generation sites, long forms, vague headlines, slow pages and missing proof do the same damage less visibly. Speed alone matters: Deloitte's study for Google linked a 0.1 second mobile speed gain to an 8.4 percent lift in retail conversions.

CRO is not guessing at button colours. It is finding out why people hesitate and removing the reason.

N° 02

How we find what to fix

Analytics

We check that GA4 tracks every meaningful action correctly, then map where visitors drop out: which landing pages, devices, sources and steps lose the most people.

Behaviour

Session recordings and heatmaps show where people scroll, click, hesitate and give up. Form analytics show which fields cause abandonment.

Voice of the customer

Short on-site surveys, interviews with recent buyers and sales call notes reveal the questions and doubts that pages fail to answer.

Expert review

A structured review of each key page against clarity, relevance, trust, friction and motivation, using established usability research.

N° 03

Changes that most often move the number

  • A headline that says what you do and for whom, in the visitor's words.
  • Proof next to every claim: reviews, case details, certifications, guarantees, real photos.
  • Shorter forms, asking only what you need to reply, with clear error messages.
  • Answers to objections on the page: price ranges, timelines, process, what happens next.
  • Faster pages, especially on mobile and on paid landing pages.
  • A single clear next step above the fold and repeated where decisions happen.
  • Checkout clean-up for stores: guest checkout, clear delivery costs early, trusted payment options including UPI.

The process

Our testing process

  1. Measure Fix tracking and set a reliable baseline.
  2. Research Analytics, recordings, surveys and expert review.
  3. Hypothesise A clear statement of what we will change, why, and what we expect.
  4. Test A/B test where traffic allows; careful before and after comparison where it does not.
  5. Learn and roll out Ship winners, record what we learned, move to the next idea.

N° 06

Testing honestly

We do not stop a test the day it looks good, and we do not report a lift that the data does not support. A test that shows no difference is still useful: it tells you where not to spend more effort.

FAQ

Questions about CRO

How much traffic do we need for A/B testing?

It depends on your current conversion rate and the size of change you want to detect, but most tests need a few hundred conversions per version to be reliable. Lower-traffic sites still benefit from research-led fixes without formal testing.

What is a good conversion rate?

It varies widely by industry, offer, price and traffic source, so averages are rarely useful. The best benchmark is your own rate over time, by page and by channel.

Will CRO change our brand or design?

Only where it helps. We work within your brand and make changes that clarify, not ones that cheapen it.

How quickly will we see results?

Tracking fixes and obvious friction can lift results within weeks. A steady testing programme compounds over months.
